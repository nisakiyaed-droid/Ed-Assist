# Session 1 — Parts of a Plant

## PART ONE · Before Class *(prepare; all elements equal)*

**Learning Outcomes** *(by the end, children will be able to…)*
1. Name the five main parts of a plant — root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit. *(knowledge)*
2. Say what each part does for the plant, in one simple sentence. *(understanding)*
3. Point out these parts on a real plant or a clear picture. *(skill)*
4. Explain why a plant needs its roots and leaves to stay alive. *(thinking)*
5. Show care when handling a real plant, treating it gently as a living thing. *(values)*

**Prerequisites Check** — Children should know that plants are living things and that they grow. A quick question at the start confirms this.

**Anticipated Misconceptions** *(exactly three)*
1. *Child says:* "The roots are dirty, they don't do anything." — *Why:* roots are hidden underground, so their job is invisible. — *Teacher does:* shows a plant with roots in a clear glass of water; "the roots drink the water for the whole plant."
2. *Child says:* "Only the flower is the important part." — *Why:* flowers are bright and noticed most. — *Teacher does:* covers the flower and asks, "can the plant still live?" then covers the leaves and asks again.
3. *Child says:* "The stem is just a stick." — *Why:* the stem looks plain and still. — *Teacher does:* traces water's path up the stem with a finger; "the stem is the plant's straw and its backbone."

**Anticipated Student Questions** *(with ready responses)*
1. "Do all plants have flowers?" → "Not all, and not always. Some show flowers only in one season, and a few never do — but most have roots, a stem, and leaves."
2. "Why are leaves green?" → "Leaves hold a green colour that helps them catch sunlight to make the plant's food. We'll meet that idea again in a higher class."
3. "Can a plant live without roots?" → "No — without roots it cannot drink water or hold itself in the soil. Let's see why with our glass-of-water plant."
4. "Is a fruit a part of the plant?" → "Yes! The fruit grows from the flower and carries the seeds — the plant's babies."
5. "Why does my plant at home droop?" → "Often it is thirsty — its roots have no water to send up. What do you think it needs?"

**Questioning Technique Guide**
- Q1–2 (closed, with a visual): hold up the plant — "Point to the root. Point to a leaf."
- Q3–4 (think time + think-pair-share): "Tell your partner which part you think is most important, and why."
- Q5 (Socratic): "If a plant could talk, what would each part say its job is?"

**Differentiation**
- *Support:* give a labelled picture card so the child matches the word to the part.
- *Extension:* ask the child to add one more part not in the book (e.g. a bud) and say what it might become.

## PART TWO · During Class — Enhanced sequence *(~35 min)*

1. **Prior Knowledge Activation** *(3 min)* — Hold up the real plant. "What is this? Is it living? How do you know?" Let three children answer. *(Session 1, so no contingency note from a previous misconception.)*
2. **Hook** *(3 min)* — "I have a plant here that is drinking water right now — and you can see it drink. Want to see how?" Show the roots in the clear glass.
3. **Teach the Concept** *(8 min)* — Name each part on the real plant, then the diagram: root (drinks and holds), stem (carries and stands), leaf (makes food, breathes), flower (makes fruit), fruit (holds seeds). One clean sentence per part. Check understanding after each.
4. **Class Activity** *(6 min)* — Children come up in pairs to point to and name a part on the plant; the class gives a thumbs-up if correct. Hand support-cards to those who need them.
5. **Assessment for Learning — three modes** *(5 min)* —
   - *Written:* draw a plant and label any three parts.
   - *Oral:* "Tell me one thing the root does."
   - *Physical:* place the picture cards in order from underground to top (root → stem → leaf → flower → fruit).
6. **The Story** *(7 min)* — *Read "Meena and Paati's Garden" (below). It ends with a line that answers our question and one that hangs for tomorrow.*
7. **Takeaway** *(3 min)* — Show Today's Learning Chart. Children finish the sentence: *"Today I learned that a plant has…"*

> **Story · Meena and Paati's Garden**
>
> In a village near Madurai, where the morning smells of jasmine and filter coffee, lived a girl named Meena. Every evening she helped her grandmother — her Paati — in the little garden behind their house.
>
> One evening Meena saw Paati gently pouring water at the *bottom* of the tomato plant, near the soil. "Paati, why there? The leaves are up here!" Paati smiled. "Ah, but the plant does not drink with its leaves, kanna. It drinks with its roots — hidden under the soil, like quiet little straws."
>
> Meena bent down. She could not see the roots, but she imagined them sipping. "And the stem?" she asked. "The stem is the plant's back — it stands tall and carries the water up to every leaf. The leaves make the plant's food in the sunshine. The flower will become a fruit. And inside the fruit…" Paati pressed a tomato seed into Meena's palm. "…sleep the plant's little babies."
>
> Meena looked at the whole plant — root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit — working together like her own family at dinner. *"So a plant has many parts, and each one has its own job,"* she whispered.
>
> That night, Meena wondered: *if her one tomato plant was a whole little world… how many kinds of plants were waiting in the village for her to meet tomorrow?*

### Full sequence *(~40 min — Enhanced plus two)*

- **Did You Know?** *(1 min)* — "The world's largest flower has no stem or leaves of its own — and it smells like old socks!"
