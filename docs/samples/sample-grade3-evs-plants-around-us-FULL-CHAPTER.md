# Session 2 — Kinds of Plants

> **Chapter Progress So Far:** In Session 1, children learned a plant's five parts — root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit — and that each has a job. Meena discovered this in Paati's garden and went to bed wondering how many *kinds* of plants waited in her village. Today she finds out.

## PART ONE · Before Class *(prepare; all elements equal)*

**Learning Outcomes**
1. Name the five kinds of plants — trees, shrubs, herbs, creepers, climbers. *(knowledge)*
2. Describe one clear feature of each kind. *(understanding)*
3. Sort real or pictured plants into the right kind. *(skill)*
4. Explain why a creeper and a tree grow so differently. *(thinking)*
5. Notice the variety of plants around them with interest and respect. *(values)*

**Prerequisites Check** — Children should recall the five plant parts from Session 1. A one-question recap confirms it.

**Anticipated Misconceptions** *(exactly three)*
1. *Child says:* "Small plants are baby trees." — *Why:* size is the obvious clue. — *Teacher does:* shows a full-grown mint herb beside a tree sapling; "this herb is fully grown and will stay small — size is not age."
2. *Child says:* "A creeper and a climber are the same." — *Why:* both have weak stems. — *Teacher does:* mimes with hands — creeper spreads *along the ground*, climber *goes up* with support.
3. *Child says:* "Only trees are real plants." — *Why:* trees are biggest and most noticed. — *Teacher does:* lines up a tree, a rose shrub, a mint herb — "all plants, different kinds."

**Anticipated Student Questions**
1. "Is grass a plant?" → "Yes — grass is a herb. Soft green stem, stays low."
2. "Is a coconut tree a tree?" → "Yes, the tallest kind — one strong woody trunk."
3. "Why does the money plant climb the wall?" → "It is a climber — a weak stem that needs support to reach the light."
4. "Can a shrub become a tree?" → "No — a shrub stays bushy with many woody stems near the ground; a tree grows one tall trunk."
5. "Which kind is the tomato plant from yesterday?" → "Good thinking back! It is a herb — soft stem, stays small."

**Questioning Technique Guide** — Q1–2 closed with picture prompts; Q3–4 think-pair-share; Q5 Socratic ("If you were a seed by a wall, would you rather creep or climb? Why?").

**Differentiation** — *Support:* a sorting mat with the five kinds pictured. *Extension:* find a plant that is hard to classify and argue which kind it is.

## PART TWO · During Class — Enhanced sequence *(~35 min)*

1. **Prior Knowledge Activation** *(3 min)* — "Yesterday — what are the five parts of a plant?" *Contingency (predicted from Session 1's misconception): if a child still says roots "do nothing," touch back to the glass-of-water plant for ten seconds, then move on.*
2. **Hook** *(3 min)* — Pass round the mint and the shrub twig. "Same plant? Why not?"
3. **Teach the Concept** *(8 min)* — Five kinds, one clear feature each: tree (one tall woody trunk), shrub (bushy, many woody stems, low), herb (soft green stem, small), creeper (weak stem, spreads on the ground), climber (weak stem, climbs with support). Check after each.
4. **Class Activity — sorting** *(7 min)* — Children sort the picture cards onto the five kinds; pairs check each other.
5. **Assessment for Learning — three modes** *(5 min)* — *Written:* match four plants to their kind. *Oral:* "Tell me how a creeper differs from a climber." *Physical:* stand and mime each kind (tall tree, bushy shrub, low herb, creeping hands, climbing hands).
6. **The Story** *(6 min)* — *Read "Meena's Village Walk" (below).*
7. **Takeaway** *(3 min)* — Show Today's Learning Chart. Children finish: *"I can tell a tree from a creeper because…"*

> **Story · Meena's Village Walk** *(≈210 words)*
>
> The next morning, Meena could not wait. With Paati's hand in hers, she set out to meet the plants of her village near Madurai.
>
> First came the giant by the temple — the old peepal **tree**, one great trunk, tall enough to shade the whole courtyard where the morning kolam was drawn. "One strong trunk, reaching high," said Paati. "That is a tree."
>
> By the gate stood the jasmine **shrub**, bushy and low, many small woody stems crowded with white buds for the evening prayers. "Not one trunk, but many — and it stays small. A shrub," smiled Paati.
>
> Near the well, soft green **herbs** — mint and tulsi — brushed Meena's ankles. "Soft stems, close to the ground. Herbs," she said, remembering yesterday's tomato.
>
> Then, along the low wall, a pumpkin vine **crept** flat across the earth, while beside the house a money plant **climbed** up a string toward the sun. Meena spread her fingers low like the creeper, then walked them upward like the climber, and laughed.
>
> *So the village held not one kind of plant but five, each growing in its own way.*
>
> Walking home, Meena passed Paati's garden and noticed her snip a yellow leaf and tuck it into the soil, wasting nothing. *Why,* Meena wondered, *does Paati treasure every single plant?*

# Session 3 — What Plants Give Us

> **Chapter Progress So Far:** Children know the five plant parts (Session 1) and the five kinds of plants (Session 2). Meena has met her village's plants and noticed Paati treasures every one. Today she — and the class — discover *why*.

## PART ONE · Before Class

**Learning Outcomes**
1. List at least four things plants give us — food, air to breathe, shade, medicine. *(knowledge)*
2. Give one real example for each gift. *(understanding)*
3. Sort everyday things into "comes from a plant" or "does not." *(skill)*
4. Explain why life would be hard without plants. *(thinking)*
5. Show a wish to protect plants, not waste them. *(values)*

**Prerequisites Check** — Children recall that there are many kinds of plants all around them.

**Anticipated Misconceptions** *(exactly three)*
1. *Child says:* "Food comes from shops, not plants." — *Why:* children meet food already packaged. — *Teacher does:* traces rice → paddy field → plant; "the shop only carried it from the plant."
2. *Child says:* "Plants are not important, only animals are." — *Why:* plants seem still and silent. — *Teacher does:* "Hold your breath… plants made that air you just needed."
3. *Child says:* "Medicine comes only from a factory." — *Why:* medicine looks man-made. — *Teacher does:* holds tulsi/neem; "Paati's cough kashayam begins with leaves like these."

**Anticipated Student Questions**
1. "Do plants really make the air?" → "Yes — leaves give out the oxygen we breathe. A big reason to keep trees."
2. "Is sugar from a plant?" → "Yes — from sugarcane, tall and sweet, grown right here in Tamil Nadu."
3. "Which plant gives medicine?" → "Many — tulsi, neem, ginger. People have used them for hundreds of years."
4. "Why do we sit under trees in summer?" → "For shade — the leaves block the hot sun and the air feels cooler."
5. "What if all the plants were gone?" → "No food, no fresh air, no shade — that is why we protect them. What could *you* do?"

**Questioning Technique Guide** — Q1–2 closed with real objects; Q3–4 think-pair-share; Q5 Socratic ("If you could thank a plant, what would you thank it for?").

**Differentiation** — *Support:* a picture set to sort into "from a plant / not." *Extension:* name a plant gift not in the book (paper, cotton, rubber) and trace it back.

**Teaching Aids Checklist**

## PART TWO · During Class — Enhanced sequence *(~35 min)*

1. **Prior Knowledge Activation** *(3 min)* — "Yesterday Meena saw Paati waste no plant. Any guesses why plants matter so much?" *Contingency (from Session 2's misconception): if a child still says small plants are baby trees, a five-second reminder — "herbs stay small on purpose" — then move on.*
2. **Hook** *(3 min)* — "Everyone, hold your breath… now breathe. A plant made that breath. Let me show you the other gifts plants give." 
3. **Teach the Concept** *(8 min)* — Four gifts with a real example each: **food** (rice from paddy, fruit, vegetables), **air** (leaves give oxygen), **shade** (the peepal at the temple), **medicine** (tulsi, neem). Check after each.
4. **Class Activity — "From a plant?"** *(6 min)* — Children sort real items and picture cards into "from a plant / not from a plant"; discuss the surprises (cotton shirt — yes!).
5. **Assessment for Learning — three modes** *(5 min)* — *Written:* write two things plants give us. *Oral:* "Tell me one plant that gives medicine." *Physical:* hold up the real items and place each on the right side of the sorting line.
6. **The Story** *(7 min)* — *Read "Why Paati Treasures Plants" (below).*
7. **Takeaway** *(3 min)* — Today's Learning Chart. Children finish: *"Plants are important because…"*

> **Story · Why Paati Treasures Plants** *(≈215 words)*
>
> That evening, Meena finally asked it. "Paati, why do you never waste even a yellow leaf?"
>
> Paati sat her down on the veranda, the smell of rice cooking inside. "Look at your plate at lunch today, kanna. The rice — from the paddy plants that turn our fields green. The vegetables — from plants in this very garden. Even the sugar in your evening payasam comes from sugarcane, tall as your father."
>
> Meena's eyes widened. "So my food *is* plants?"
>
> "Most of it," Paati smiled. "And more. Take a deep breath — that fresh air is the gift of leaves. When the sun burns at noon, where do we run? Under the peepal tree's shade. And when your throat hurt last week, what did I give you?"
>
> "Your tulsi kashayam," Meena remembered.
>
> "Medicine, from leaves," Paati nodded. "Food, air, shade, medicine — the plants give and give and ask for so little. A little water. A little care."
>
> Meena looked at the garden with new eyes. Every plant was quietly giving her something, all day long. *No wonder Paati treasured them all.*
>
> She picked up the watering can herself. *If plants give us so much,* she thought, *could I grow one of my very own — and give a little back?*

### Core sequence *(~25 min)*
Prior Knowledge Activation → Teach the Concept → Class Activity → Assessment for Learning → The Story → Takeaway.

### Full sequence *(~40 min — Enhanced plus two)*

- **Did You Know?** *(1 min)* — "The oxygen in one good breath you take was made by plants — a single large tree can make enough air for two people for a whole day!"

# Session 4 — Caring for Plants  ·  ★ STORY FINALE

> **Chapter Progress So Far:** Children now know a plant's parts (Session 1), the kinds of plants (Session 2), and the gifts plants give us (Session 3). Meena has gone from a curious girl in Paati's garden to one who wants to grow a plant of her own. Today everything comes together — and Meena's story reaches its finale.

## PART ONE · Before Class

**Learning Outcomes**
1. State what a plant needs to grow — water, sunlight, air, soil. *(knowledge)*
2. Describe two simple ways to care for a plant. *(understanding)*
3. Demonstrate planting/caring steps in the right order. *(skill)*
4. Explain why caring for plants helps people, animals, and the Earth. *(thinking — synthesis)*
5. Commit to one caring action of their own. *(values)*

**Prerequisites Check** — Children can recall, from across the chapter, parts, kinds, and gifts of plants. This session weaves all three together.

**Anticipated Misconceptions** *(exactly three)*
1. *Child says:* "More water is always better." — *Why:* water seems purely good. — *Teacher does:* "Too much water drowns the roots — remember the roots breathe too. A little, often."
2. *Child says:* "Plants do not need us; they grow on their own." — *Why:* wild plants seem to manage. — *Teacher does:* compares a watered garden plant with a thirsty, drooping one.
3. *Child says:* "Only big gardens matter." — *Why:* one small plant feels too little. — *Teacher does:* "One plant gives one family shade, food, or air — and every forest began as one."

**Anticipated Student Questions**
1. "How much water does a plant need?" → "Enough to keep the soil damp, not flooded. The roots like a drink, not a bath."
2. "Why does my plant lean to the window?" → "It reaches for sunlight — its food is made in the light."
3. "Can I grow a plant in my house?" → "Yes! A herb like mint or tulsi grows happily in a small pot."
4. "What if I forget to water it?" → "It will droop to remind you. Forgive yourself, water gently, and it often revives."
5. "Why should we plant trees?" → "For all the gifts we learned — food, air, shade, medicine — for us and for those who come after us."

**Questioning Technique Guide** — Q1–2 closed with the real sapling; Q3–4 think-pair-share; Q5 Socratic ("If you plant a tree you may never sit under, why do it?").

**Differentiation** — *Support:* sequence cards for "how to plant a sapling" to arrange. *Extension:* plan how they would care for a plant for one whole week.

## PART TWO · During Class — Enhanced sequence *(~35 min)*

1. **Prior Knowledge Activation** *(3 min)* — "This week we learned a plant's parts, its kinds, and its gifts. Today — how do we care for one?" *Contingency (from Session 3): if a child still thinks food comes only from shops, a five-second "rice → paddy" reminder, then on.*
2. **Hook** *(2 min)* — Hold up the sapling. "This little one is ready to grow. Shall we help it — and hear how Meena helped hers?"
3. **Teach the Concept** *(7 min)* — What a plant needs: **water, sunlight, air, soil.** Two care habits: water gently and regularly; give it light. Check understanding.
4. **Class Activity — plant it together** *(6 min)* — As a class, plant the sapling in the right order (dig, place, cover, water, set in light); children call out each step.
5. **Assessment for Learning — three modes** *(5 min)* — *Written:* write two things a plant needs. *Oral:* "Tell me one way to care for a plant." *Physical:* arrange the sequence cards in the correct planting order.
6. **The Story — STORY FINALE** *(9 min)* — *Read "Meena's Own Little Plant" (below). It gathers the whole chapter and closes Meena's journey.*
7. **Takeaway** *(3 min)* — Today's Learning Chart. Children finish: *"The most important thing about plants is…"*

> **★ STORY FINALE · Meena's Own Little Plant** *(≈360 words)*
>
> On Sunday morning, Paati placed something in Meena's hands: a tiny tulsi sapling, no taller than a pencil. "It is yours now, kanna. Will you care for it?"
>
> Meena's heart jumped. She remembered everything from her week of wondering. She looked at the little plant and named its **parts** — the thread-thin **roots**, the soft green **stem**, the first few **leaves**. "A herb," she said proudly, recalling the **kinds** of plants — not a tree, not a creeper, but a small soft herb, just like the mint by the well.
>
> "Where shall I plant it?" she asked.
>
> "Where it will get what it needs," said Paati. So Meena thought of what plants need — **soil, water, sunlight, air.** She chose a sunny corner of the veranda, dug a little hole, settled the roots gently, covered them with soil, and gave just a little water. "Not too much," she whispered, "the roots must breathe."
>
> Each morning that week, Meena visited her tulsi. She gave it a small drink and turned it to the light. And as it grew, she understood why Paati treasured every plant — for one day this tiny herb would offer its **gifts**: fresh **air** from its leaves, **medicine** in Paati's kashayam, perhaps even seeds for new plants.
>
> By Friday, a brand-new leaf had unfurled. Meena ran to Paati, eyes shining. "Paati! It grew! I helped something live!"
>
> Paati pulled her close. "You did more than that, kanna. You learned the secret of every gardener — that the more gently we care for plants, the more they care for us."
>
> Meena looked from her little tulsi to Paati's whole green garden, and at last she understood the most important thing of all: *plants give us life, and when we care for them, we care for ourselves and for everyone who will come after us.*
>
> That evening, Meena tucked a single tulsi seed into her pocket — and smiled. *Tomorrow,* she thought, *I will plant another. And maybe, one day, a whole garden of my own.*

# Chapter Summary — *Plants Around Us* (Grade 3 EVS)

## For the teacher *(quick reference / revision before assessment)*

| Session | Big idea | Key words | Assessment focus |
|--------:|----------|-----------|------------------|
| 1 | A plant has 5 **parts**, each with a job | root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit | Name & point to parts |
| 2 | There are 5 **kinds** of plants | tree, shrub, herb, creeper, climber | Sort plants by kind |
| 3 | Plants give us **gifts** | food, air, shade, medicine | Give an example per gift |
| 4 | Plants need care; how to **grow** one | water, sunlight, soil, care, sapling | Care steps in order |

**Whole-chapter outcomes:** children can name the parts and kinds of plants, explain what plants give us, describe how to care for a plant, and show a respectful attitude toward living things. **Story thread:** Meena, guided by Paati, journeys from noticing plants to growing her own — parts → kinds → gifts → care, ending in synthesis.

> 🌳 **Plants Around Us — what I learned**
>
> - A plant has **5 parts**: root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit. Each has a job!
> - There are **5 kinds**: tree, shrub, herb, creeper, climber.
> - Plants give us **food, air, shade, and medicine.**
> - Plants need **water, sunlight, air, and soil** — and our **care.**
> - Like **Meena**, I can grow a plant of my own and care for it gently. 🌱
