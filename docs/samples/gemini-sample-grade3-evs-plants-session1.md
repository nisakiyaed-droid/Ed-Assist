# Session 1: Introducing Plants Around Us

---

## Part One — Pre-Class Preparation

**Learning Outcomes**
By the end of this session, students will be able to:
*   Identify and name three main types of plants: trees, shrubs, and herbs.
*   Classify common plants into categories based on their size and stem characteristics.
*   Appreciate the diverse forms of plant life found in their local environment.
*   Show curiosity about how different plants grow and where they are found.

**Prerequisites Check**
Students should be able to:
*   Recognise common plants in their surroundings (e.g., mango tree, rose plant).
*   Understand that plants are living things and are found everywhere.
*   Identify basic plant parts like leaves and stem.

**Anticipated Misconceptions**

| What the child says                               | Why they might say it                                   | What the teacher does                                                               |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "All plants are the same, just different sizes."  | Limited exposure to diverse plant structures.           | Show contrasting pictures of trees, shrubs, and herbs, highlighting unique features. |
| "Only big plants like trees are important."       | Big trees are more noticeable and provide obvious benefits like shade or fruit. | Discuss the importance of small plants, such as herbs used in cooking or medicine. |
| "Plants are boring because they don't move."      | Observation of plants as static objects.                | Briefly mention how plants grow, turn towards the sun, or produce flowers and fruits. |

**Anticipated Student Questions**
1.  **"What is the biggest tree in the world?"**
    *   **Response:** That's a great question! Some trees, like the giant sequoias, can grow incredibly tall and wide, but today, let's focus on the trees we see every day around us.
2.  **"Do all plants have flowers?"**
    *   **Response:** Most plants you see do have flowers, even if they are very tiny. Flowers are how many plants make new seeds to grow more plants!
3.  **"Can plants grow inside our houses?"**
    *   **Response:** Yes, definitely! Many plants, like money plants or snake plants, are happy to grow inside our homes where they get some light and care.
4.  **"How does a tiny seed become a huge tree?"**
    *   **Response:** That's a magical process called germination and growth! With water, sunlight, and good soil, a tiny seed can grow roots and a shoot, slowly becoming bigger and bigger over many years.

**Questioning Technique Guide**
1.  **Closed with visual prompts:** "Look at this picture. Is this plant tall or short?" (Show a picture of a tree vs. a herb).
2.  **Think time and think-pair-share:** "What differences do you notice between a rose plant and a mango tree? Think for a moment, then discuss with your partner."
3.  **Socratic:** "Why do you think some plants have very strong, woody stems, while others have soft, green stems? What might be the advantage of each?"

**Differentiation**
*   **Support:** Provide pre-sorted picture cards of plants for students to match to "Tree," "Shrub," or "Herb" labels, rather than open sorting. Use gestures for "tall" (tree), "bushy" (shrub), "small" (herb) during discussion.
*   **Extension:** Ask students to draw and label three different plants they saw on their way to school, categorising each as a tree, shrub, or herb, and explaining their reasoning.

**Teaching Aids Checklist**
*   **Pictures/Flashcards:** Various plants (mango tree, coconut tree, neem tree, hibiscus, rose, jasmine, tulsi, mint, coriander) – serves Teach the Concept, Class Activity, Assessment for Learning.
*   **Actual small plants:** A small potted herb (e.g., tulsi or mint) and a small potted shrub (e.g., small rose plant) – serves Teach the Concept, Assessment for Learning (physical).
*   **Chart paper/Whiteboard:** For writing definitions and sorting activity – serves Teach the Concept, Class Activity.
*   **Markers:** For writing and drawing.
*   **Today's Learning Chart (A4):** A printed chart with "Plants come in many different shapes and sizes!" (green), "Tree, Shrub, Herb" (blue), and an icon showing a tall tree, a bushy shrub, and a small herb (orange) – serves Takeaway.

**Choose the Level**
Enhanced. This level provides a comprehensive introduction to the topic by including a Hook to spark curiosity, a dedicated Vocabulary Moment to clarify new terms, and a varied Assessment for Learning to check understanding thoroughly, all within the target time.

## Part Two — In-Class Execution

**Enhanced Sequence** (Approx. 35 minutes)

1.  **Prior Knowledge Activation (5 min)**
    *   "Good morning, everyone! Let's start by thinking about the amazing green world around us. What plants do you see every day on your way to school? Can you name some plants you have in your garden or even inside your home?"
    *   *Contingency: Not applicable for Session 1.*

2.  **Hook (3 min)**
    *   "Imagine you are tiny ants walking through a giant garden. What amazing structures would you see? Would everything look the same, or would you find towering giants, bushy homes, and soft, grassy paths?"
    *   *(Show a large, colourful picture of a diverse garden or forest scene, prompting imagination.)*

3.  **Teach the Concept (10 min)**
    *   "Today, we are going to become plant detectives and learn about the different kinds of plants around us! We will learn about Trees, Shrubs, and Herbs."
    *   "**Trees** are usually very tall and big. They have a single, thick, woody stem called a trunk, and branches that grow high above the ground. Think of a big mango tree or a coconut tree." *(Show pictures of trees, point out trunk and branches.)*
    *   "**Shrubs** are smaller than trees. They have many woody stems that grow close to the ground, making them look bushy. A rose plant or a hibiscus plant are good examples of shrubs." *(Show pictures of shrubs, point out multiple stems from the base.)*
    *   "**Herbs** are the smallest of these plants. They have soft, green, and delicate stems. They usually don't live for a very long time. Mint, coriander, and tulsi are all herbs." *(Show pictures or a real example of a herb, gently touch its stem to show softness.)*
    *   "So, trees are tall with one main trunk, shrubs are medium-sized and bushy with many stems, and herbs are small with soft stems."

4.  **Vocabulary Moment (3 min)**
    *   "Let's learn some important words to describe our plants today."
    *   "**Tree:** A large plant with a single, woody trunk." *(Write 'Tree' on the board.)*
    *   "**Shrub:** A medium-sized plant with many woody stems from the ground." *(Write 'Shrub'.)*
    *   "**Herb:** A small plant with a soft, green stem." *(Write 'Herb'.)*
    *   "**Stem:** The main support of a plant, carrying water and nutrients." *(Point to stems on real plants/pictures.)*
    *   "**Branch:** A part of a tree or shrub that grows out from the main stem." *(Point to branches.)*
    *   "Let's say these words together!" *(Choral repetition.)*

5.  **Class Activity (7 min)**
    *   **"Plant Sort!"**
    *   "I have many pictures of different plants. In your groups, I want you to sort these pictures into three categories: Tree, Shrub, or Herb. Discuss with your team why you are placing each plant in that category, thinking about its size and stem."
    *   *(Distribute sets of plant pictures to small groups. Have them stick the pictures onto pre-labelled chart papers or draw columns on their notebooks.)*

6.  **Assessment for Learning (5 min)**
    *   **Written:** "On your slate or in your notebook, draw one example of a tree, one shrub, and one herb. Write the name of each plant next to your drawing."
    *   **Oral:** "Can someone tell me, in your own words, what is the main difference between a shrub and a herb? What about a tree and a shrub?"
    *   **Physical/Diagram-based:** "I will call out plant names. If it's a **tree**, stand up tall. If it's a **shrub**, half-squat and make your arms bushy. If it's a **herb**, sit down quietly. Ready?" (Call out: Mango tree, Rose bush, Mint plant, Coconut tree, Tulsi, Hibiscus).

7.  **The Story (6 min)**
    *   "Let me tell you a story about a girl named Rani and her garden."
    *   Rani woke up to the sound of chirping birds. She loved her garden in Coimbatore. Today, she decided to explore it properly. First, she looked up at the giant mango tree. Its thick trunk reached so high, providing cool shade. "Wow, you are so tall!" she whispered. Then, she walked past the bright red hibiscus bush. Its branches were many, growing close to the ground, and it was full of beautiful flowers. Finally, near the kitchen door, she saw her mother's small tulsi plant. It was tiny, with soft green leaves, growing straight from a delicate stem. Rani knelt down, touching its leaves gently. "So many different plants, right here!" she thought.
    *   *The essential question answered: Rani found many different types of plants right in her own garden, each with its own size and stem.*
    *   *Follow-on question: "How many different kinds of plants do you think Rani will find if she looks even closer in her garden tomorrow?"*

8.  **Today's Learning Chart (1 min)**
    *   *(Display the A4-sized "Today's Learning Chart".)*
    *   "Look at our chart for today! It reminds us that **Plants come in many different shapes and sizes!** We learned about **Trees, Shrubs, and Herbs**." *(Point to the words and icon.)*

9.  **Takeaway (3 min)**
    *   "Wonderful job, plant detectives! Today, we discovered that plants are not all the same. We have magnificent **trees** with strong trunks, bushy **shrubs** with many stems, and delicate **herbs** with soft stems."
    *   "Your mission for tonight: When you go home, look at the plants around your house, on your street, or in a park. Can you find at least one tree, one shrub, and one herb? Try to tell an elder what type of plant it is and why!"

**Core Sequence** (Approx. 25 minutes)

1.  **Prior Knowledge Activation (5 min)**
    *   "What plants do you see every day on your way to school? Can you name some plants you have in your garden or even inside your home?"
2.  **Teach the Concept (8 min)**
    *   "Today, we are going to learn about the different kinds of plants around us: Trees, Shrubs, and Herbs."
    *   "**Trees** are tall with a single, thick, woody trunk (e.g., mango tree). **Shrubs** are smaller, bushy plants with many woody stems from the ground (e.g., rose plant). **Herbs** are the smallest, with soft, green stems (e.g., mint, tulsi)."
    *   *(Use pictures or real examples to illustrate each type.)*
3.  **Class Activity (5 min)**
    *   **"Plant Sort!"**
    *   "In your groups, sort these plant pictures into three categories: Tree, Shrub, or Herb. Discuss your choices."
    *   *(Students sort plant pictures into categories.)*
4.  **Assessment for Learning (3 min)**
    *   **Written:** Draw one tree, one shrub, and one herb.
    *   **Oral:** Describe the difference between a shrub and a herb.
    *   **Physical:** Stand up if a tree, half-squat if a shrub, sit down if a herb (teacher calls out plants).
5.  **The Story (6 min)**
    *   Rani explored her Coimbatore garden. She saw a tall mango tree, a bushy hibiscus plant, and a small tulsi herb. She realised plants come in many different forms.
    *   *The essential question answered: Rani found many different types of plants right in her own garden, each with its own size and stem.*
    *   *Follow-on question: "How many different kinds of plants do you think Rani will find if she looks even closer in her garden tomorrow?"*
6.  **Takeaway (3 min)**
    *   "Today, we learned about trees, shrubs, and herbs. Look for these different types of plants around your house tonight!"

---

**Full Sequence** (Approx. 40 minutes)

Includes all elements of the Enhanced Sequence, plus:

*   **Cross-Curricular Link (3 min)**
    *   **English Language Arts:** "Let's use descriptive words to talk about plants! Describe the mango tree Rani saw using at least two adjectives. What about the hibiscus bush or the tulsi plant? What words help us imagine them clearly?" *(Encourage words like 'towering', 'bushy', 'delicate', 'fragrant'.)*
*   **Did You Know? (2 min)**
    *   "Did you know that not all plants fit perfectly into these three groups? For example, a banana plant looks like a tree, but it's actually a giant herb! Its stem is soft, not woody like a tree trunk. And bamboo, which can grow very tall, is actually a type of grass!"

**Read More - Session 1: Introducing Plants Around Us**

Dear Parents,

Today, we embarked on an exciting journey into the world of "Plants Around Us"! We learned that plants come in many shapes and sizes, and we focused on identifying three main types: trees, shrubs, and herbs.

**Our Story Today:**
Rani woke up to the sound of chirping birds. She loved her garden in Coimbatore. Today, she decided to explore it properly. First, she looked up at the giant mango tree. Its thick trunk reached so high, providing cool shade. "Wow, you are so tall!" she whispered. Then, she walked past the bright red hibiscus bush. Its branches were many, growing close to the ground, and it was full of beautiful flowers. Finally, near the kitchen door, she saw her mother's small tulsi plant. It was tiny, with soft green leaves, growing straight from a delicate stem. Rani knelt down, touching its leaves gently. "So many different plants, right here!" she thought.

**New Words We Learned:**
*   **Tree:** A large plant with a single, woody trunk.
*   **Shrub:** A medium-sized plant with many woody stems from the ground.
*   **Herb:** A small plant with a soft, green stem.
*   **Stem:** The main support of a plant.
*   **Branch:** A part of a tree or shrub growing from the stem.

**Amazing Fact!**
Did you know that a banana plant, which looks like a small tree, is actually considered a giant herb because its stem is soft and not woody? And bamboo, despite its height, is a type of grass! The plant world is full of surprises!

We encouraged children to observe the plants around them and identify these different types. Please support them in their 'plant detective' mission!
