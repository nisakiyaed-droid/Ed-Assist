# House Style & Readability Guide

> How every downloadable plan should **look** and **read**. Plain on purpose.
> Teacher- and parent-facing text sits at about an **8th-grade reading level**.
> Children's stories and cards stay at the **child's grade**.

---

## 1. Color palette

These are the exact colors for every plan. They have strong contrast on white,
and they still work when printed in black and white (because each colored part
also has a label and an icon).

### Cue colors (from the Framework)

| Use | Name | Hex | Notes |
|-----|------|-----|-------|
| Concepts | Green | `#2E7D32` | Calm, "growing" |
| New words / vocabulary | Blue | `#1565C0` | Easy to spot |
| The Story | Purple | `#6A1B9A` | Feels special |
| Amazing fact / Did You Know | Orange | `#E65100` | Fun, warm |
| Cross-curricular link | Teal | `#00796B` | Clearly different |

### Part header banners

| Part | Banner color | Text on banner |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| Part One — Before Class | Blue `#E8F0FE` background | `#1565C0` |
| Part Two — During Class | Green `#E7F5E9` background | `#2E7D32` |
| Part Three — After Class | Purple `#F3E9F8` background | `#6A1B9A` |

### Neutrals

| Use | Hex |
|-----|-----|
| Body text (ink) | `#1F2430` |
| Soft text / captions | `#5A6480` |
| Lines and borders | `#D8DEE9` |
| Page background | `#FFFFFF` |
| Soft block background | `#F7F9FC` |

**Black-and-white rule:** color is never the *only* signal. Every colored part
also carries a word label (e.g. "The Story") and an icon, so a B&W print is just
as clear.

## 2. Page and type (how it looks)

- **Page:** A4, portrait, about 2 cm margins.
- **Body font:** one clean, easy font; about 11pt; line spacing 1.5.
- **Headings:** clear sizes so the eye finds parts fast. Colored part banners.
- **Lists and tables:** used often, so nothing feels like a wall of text.
- **White space:** generous. Short paragraphs. No cramping.
- **Story text:** a touch larger and warmer, so it reads aloud well.
- **Footer:** chapter, session, version, and date on every page.

---

## 3. Reading level (how it reads)

### Teacher- and parent-facing text → about 8th grade

This covers the plan's instructions, the reflection questions, and the Evening
Post. Rules:

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Everyday words. If a hard word is needed, explain it once.
- No short-forms. Always write words in full ("Assessment for Learning").
- Active voice. "Show the plant," not "the plant should be shown."
- Aim for a Flesch-Kincaid grade of **8 or lower** for this text.

### Child-facing text → the child's grade

This covers the **story**, the **Today's Learning Chart**, and the **child
revision card**. This text is for the children, so it matches their grade:

- Grades 1–2: very short, warm, lots of repeating.
- Grade 3: simple sentences, like the Meena stories.
- Grades 4–5: a bit longer, more real-world.

> **The one-line rule:** Grown-up text is 8th-grade plain. Kid text is kid-level.
> Never make a 7-year-old read 8th-grade words, and never make a teacher wade
> through jargon.

### How we check it

1. **Read-back rule (already in the Framework):** after writing a story or a set
   of questions, read it again against the grade and fix anything too hard.
2. **Plain-word check:** swap any big word for a smaller one that means the same.
3. **Sentence-length check:** if a sentence runs long, cut it in two.
